# Research Synthesis

Turn raw user evidence into traceable design findings without converting a handful of anecdotes into unsupported certainty.

## Inputs

May include:
- interview transcripts;
- usability tests;
- survey responses;
- support tickets;
- reviews;
- NPS/CSAT feedback;
- product analytics notes;
- observation logs.

Preserve the distinction between:
- what participants said;
- what participants did;
- interpretation;
- recommendation.

## Process

### 1. Frame the study
Identify:
- research question;
- method;
- participant/sample context;
- known limitations.

### 2. Extract evidence
Capture:
- repeated tasks;
- pain points;
- workarounds;
- goals;
- vocabulary;
- decision factors;
- confusion;
- delight;
- unmet needs.

### 3. Cluster
Create themes only when multiple pieces of evidence support them.

Do not force every observation into a theme.

### 4. Distinguish strength
Label findings by evidence strength, for example:
- strong repeated pattern;
- moderate pattern;
- isolated signal;
- contradiction;
- unknown.

### 5. Convert to design implications
For each meaningful finding:
- evidence;
- interpretation;
- affected user/task;
- design implication;
- recommended next step.

A design implication is not automatically a feature request.

### 6. Preserve contradictions
When participants disagree, report the split and investigate whether it correlates with role, experience, context, or task.

## Output

Include:
- executive synthesis;
- research questions/method;
- top themes;
- supporting evidence;
- contradictions;
- opportunity areas;
- prioritized design implications;
- open questions;
- recommended validation.

Avoid fake precision when the sample does not support it.
